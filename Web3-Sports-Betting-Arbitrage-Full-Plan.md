## 2. What Is Betting Arbitrage?

### How It Works

Betting arbitrage occurs when different bookmakers offer different odds on the same event, creating a situation where you can bet on ALL possible outcomes and still make a profit.

**Example:**
```
Lakers vs Celtics — Moneyline

Bookmaker A: Lakers win @ 2.10 (implied probability 47.6%)
Bookmaker B: Celtics win @ 2.10 (implied probability 47.6%)

Total implied probability: 95.2% (under 100% = ARBITRAGE EXISTS)
```

If you bet $10 on Lakers @ 2.10 and $10 on Celtics @ 2.10:
- If Lakers win: You get $21 (profit $1)
- If Celtics win: You get $21 (profit $1)
- **Guaranteed profit: $1 on $20 staked = 5% ROI**

### Types of Arbitrage

| Type | Description | Typical ROI | Difficulty |
|------|-------------|-------------|------------|
| **2-way (Moneyline)** | Two outcomes (tennis, basketball, UFC) | 1-5% | Easy |
| **3-way (1X2)** | Three outcomes (soccer: win/draw/win) | 2-8% | Medium |
| **Cross-platform Web3** | Same event on Polymarket vs sportsbook | 3-10% | Medium |
| **Live/In-play** | Odds shift during live game | 5-15% | Hard |
| **Bonus exploitation** | Using free bets and deposit matches | 50-100%+ | Easy |

## 7. Web3-Specific Arbitrage Techniques

### Technique 1: Polymarket Cross-Arbitrage

**How it works:** Polymarket prices sports outcomes as shares (YES/NO at $0.XX each). The share price directly maps to decimal odds: a $0.50 YES share = 2.00 decimal odds.

**Why arbs exist:** Polymarket's order book is driven by retail crowd sentiment, while sportsbooks use professional odds compilers. These two pricing mechanisms regularly disagree by 3-8%.

**Execution:**
```
1. Find a sports event listed on both Polymarket and a sportsbook
2. Convert Polymarket share price to decimal odds: odds = 1 / share_price
3. Compare with sportsbook odds
4. If implied probabilities sum to < 100%, you have an arb
5. Buy the cheaper side on Polymarket, bet the other side on the sportsbook
```

**Example:**
```
NBA: Lakers vs Celtics

Polymarket: Lakers YES = $0.45 → Odds = 1/0.45 = 2.22
Sportsbook: Celtics @ 2.20

Implied probability = 1/2.22 + 1/2.20 = 0.4505 + 0.4545 = 0.905 (90.5%)
Arbitrage exists! Profit = 9.5%

Bet $10 on Lakers @ 2.22 on Polymarket → Return $22.20
Bet $10.09 on Celtics @ 2.20 on Sportsbook → Return $22.20
Total staked: $20.09
Guaranteed return: $22.20
Profit: $2.11 (10.5% ROI)
```

### Technique 2: SX Bet Zero-Fee Arbitrage

**How it works:** SX Bet charges **0% on single bets**, making it the cheapest possible leg for any arbitrage. Every arb that involves SX Bet as one side is more profitable.

**Strategy:** Always check SX Bet odds first for any event. If SX Bet has competitive odds, use it as one leg — the 0% fee means you keep more profit.

### Technique 3: Multi-Frontend Azuro Arbitrage

**How it works:** Azuro is a protocol with 40+ different frontends (apps that sit on top). Different frontends may show slightly different odds due to AMM pool states. You can arb between two Azuro frontends for the same event.

**Advantage:** Both sides are on-chain, fully decentralized, no account limits ever.

### Technique 4: Cross-Chain Odds Arbitrage

**How it works:** The same event may be priced differently on Overtime (Arbitrum), Polymarket (Polygon), and SX Bet (SX Network). Bridge small amounts between chains and arb the differences.

**Caution:** Factor in bridge fees (~$0.50-$2 per bridge). Only viable for arbs > $5 profit.

### Technique 5: News Lag Arbitrage

**How it works:** When breaking news hits (player injury, weather change, lineup announcement), traditional sportsbooks update within seconds. Polymarket and other prediction markets can take minutes to hours. During this lag, the old Polymarket price is stale — and that's your arb window.

**Execution:**
```
1. Follow real-time sports news (Twitter/X, injury reports, weather)
2. When breaking news drops, immediately check Polymarket
3. If Polymarket hasn't adjusted yet, bet on the correctly-priced side there
4. Hedge on a sportsbook that HAS updated
5. When Polymarket adjusts, you're guaranteed profit
```

## 11. Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them

### Pitfall 1: Account Gubbing (Getting Limited)

**What happens:** Traditional sportsbooks track your betting patterns. If you only bet when odds are mispriced, they flag your account and reduce your max bet to $1 or close it entirely.

**How to avoid:**
- Use Web3 platforms (Polymarket, SX Bet, Azuro) — they CANNOT limit you
- At traditional books, place "mug bets" (small bets on popular markets at standard odds) to look like a recreational bettor
- Round your bet amounts ($95 instead of $97.43)
- Don't always bet maximum stake — stay at 50-70% of max
- NEVER withdraw from soft books — withdrawing signals you're a winner
- Use Pinnacle, Betfair, and Web3 platforms for your sharp side — they never limit

### Pitfall 2: Execution Failure (One-Sided Bet)

**What happens:** You place the first leg of an arb, but by the time you try to place the second leg, the odds have changed. Now you're exposed to risk on one side.

**How to avoid:**
- Always place the slower-updating book FIRST
- Pre-fill bet slips so you only need to click confirm
- Practice execution speed — target under 30 seconds
- Have backup books ready in case one fails
- Start with small stakes until execution is reliable

### Pitfall 3: Voided Bets

**What happens:** A bookmaker voids your bet (often citing "palpable error" or "obvious mistake" in odds). If one leg is voided, you're exposed on the other.

**How to avoid:**
- Avoid arbs above 10% ROI — these are often due to bookmaker errors and likely to be voided
- Stick to 1-5% ROI arbs which are legitimate market differences
- If a bet is voided, immediately cash out or hedge the other side

### Pitfall 4: Overlooking Fees

**What happens:** You calculate an arb profit of 2%, but after platform fees, gas fees, and withdrawal fees, you actually lose money.

**How to avoid:**
- Always calculate NET profit after ALL fees
- SX Bet (0% singles) and DexWin (0% gas) are your best friends
- Factor in bridge fees when crossing chains (~$0.50-$2)
- Track every fee in your spreadsheet

### Pitfall 5: Keeping Too Much on One Platform

**What happens:** A platform gets hacked, goes insolvent, or freezes withdrawals. Your funds are gone.

**How to avoid:**
- Never keep more than 20% of bankroll on any single platform
- Withdraw profits daily to your personal wallet
- Use only established, audited protocols
- Keep emergency reserve in USDC on a hardware wallet
